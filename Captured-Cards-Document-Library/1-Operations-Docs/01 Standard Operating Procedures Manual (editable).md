## 1. Purpose, Scope, and How to Use This Manual

### 1.1 Purpose

This manual sets out how Captured! Cards runs day to day: in-store sales, buying and trading, inventory, online orders, events, release days, security, and the standards every team member is held to. Any Card Specialist can open the store, serve customers, buy cards, pack an order, or close the store the same way as everyone else, and nobody has to guess what "done" looks like.

### 1.2 Scope

The manual covers the Card Specialist role and the Manager and Owner decisions that support it. It covers the physical store, the online store and shipping, local pickup, events, and the store's community channels. It does not replace the Agreement, the Code of Conduct, or applicable law.

### 1.3 Order of precedence

1. Applicable law, including minimum employment standards. These always apply.
2. The signed Retail Employee Agreement and its Schedules.
3. This manual and the linked policies (Loss-Prevention Procedure, Social Media Guidelines, Pricing and Buylist Guide, Privacy Policy, Code of Conduct).
4. Verbal instructions. Anything that changes a limit, price, hold, or approval must be put in writing (a signed slip, or a direct message on the team channel) as the Agreement requires.

### 1.4 Conventions used

- **Procedure tables** use the same columns throughout: Step, Owner and time, Done looks like, and If it goes wrong. "If it goes wrong" names the failure signal, how to undo or pause, and who to contact.
- **5W2H boxes** summarize Who, When, Where, Why, How, and How much for each major process.
- **"Owner"** means the store owner. **"Manager"** means any store manager the Owner designates. By default, approvals and discretion rest with the Owner; a Manager approves as a secondary approver or where the Owner has designated them to, and if they disagree, the Owner's decision applies (Agreement 1.1).

### 1.5 Guiding priorities

When priorities conflict, decide in this order:

1. **People first.** Personal safety comes before merchandise. Never confront a thief or a threatening person.
2. **The store's interest.** Price, buy, and sell in the best interest of the store.
3. **The customer.** Be friendly, welcoming, and inclusive. Captured! Cards is an all-ages space for players, collectors, and families.
4. **Honesty.** Describe condition, authenticity, and grading honestly. Never knowingly buy, sell, or display counterfeit, altered, or misrepresented cards.
5. **Speed.** Be quick, but never at the cost of the first four.

### 3.2 Condition grading for raw singles

Use one set of terms across store, buylist, and online listings. When unsure, grade the lower condition and ask the Manager. Customers may inspect cards before purchase. Cards are labelled and listed with the four primary grades below (NM, LP, MP, HP/D). Plus and minus sub-grades are used only when buying, to assess a card more precisely.

| Grade | What you should see | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Near Mint (NM) | One or two tiny specks of edge whitening, or one faint surface mark visible only when tilted. No creases or bends. | Default for cards from fresh packs. Pack-fresh cards with no flaws are NM+ when buying. |
| Lightly Played (LP) | Noticeable edge and corner whitening, light scuffing, or several holo scratches. No creases. | Middle of LP, between LP+ and LP-. |
| Moderately Played (MP) | Heavy whitening, rounded corners, many scratches, or minor surface dirt. | Middle of MP, between MP+ and MP-. |
| Heavily Played / Damaged (HP/D) | Any crease, tear, water damage, ink or writing, peeling, warping, or major bend. One condition in our system. | Check tournament legality in sleeves. See 8.8 for how to price HP/D cards. |

**Buying sub-grades (plus and minus).** The tags on cards, the buylist, and online listings keep the primary grades NM, LP, MP, and HP/D. During a buy, use the plus and minus sub-grades below to assess where a card sits within its grade and to set the offer. Do not put sub-grades on the card tag or the listing.

| Sub-grade | Sells as | What you should see | Buying note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NM+ | NM | Pack-fresh. No whitening, no scratches, sharp corners, clean surface under light. | Top of NM. Use only when verified flawless. Check the centering: if it is a sought-after card and a PSA 10 contender, set it aside for review before making an offer. |
| NM- | NM | Light whitening on a few edges or corners, or one or two light surface scratches. No creases. | Low end of NM, a step below clean NM. Offer a little under the NM price. |
| LP+ | LP | Light whitening on several edges and a few light scratches; still clean at arm's length. | High end of LP, just below NM-. Offer a little above the LP price. |
| LP- | LP | Heavier whitening, slight corner wear, more visible scratches. No creases. | Low end of LP, just above MP. Offer a little under the LP price. |
| MP+ | MP | Clear wear on most edges and corners, scuffing, small dents with no crease. | High end of MP, just below LP-. Offer a little above the MP price. |
| MP- | MP | Very heavy wear, a minor bend or light indentation, borderline damaged. | Low end of MP, just above HP/D. Offer a little under the MP price. |
| HP/D | HP/D | Anything from heavy whitening on the back to heavy whitening on the front, creases, tears, water damage, or other damage. How much and how severe it is varies a lot within this one condition. | HP and Damaged are one condition in our labelling system. Price it by its actual condition: start from the system price and go down, up to 100% off. You can go past the 5% limit without approval. If it is too damaged, reject it (see 8.8). |

For how strictly to grade by language and era, and for the physical check, follow the Condition grading section of the **Pricing & Buylist Guide**. When a card sits between two grades, choose the lower one and show the seller the flaw. Check the centering too: if a card is sought after and looks like a PSA 10 contender, set it aside for review before making an offer.

### 3.3 Condition disputes

- Do not argue. Explain how the grade was reached, point to the specific wear, and let the customer inspect.
- Disputes raised after purchase are handled case by case by the Manager, using the singles rules in Section 12.
- Do not change the grade on a card just to close a sale. Within reason, correct a grade if the card was mis-conditioned, and tell the Manager. If you are overruled, record why.

### 3.4 Authenticity checks (every buy and every sale of high-value product)

| Check | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Texture and feel | Genuine cards have a consistent printed finish. Check holo and textured cards for correct pattern. |
| Font and print | Text sharpness, letter spacing, energy symbols, set symbols, and card number format consistent with a known genuine copy of the same set. |
| Colour | Saturation and borders should match a genuine reference. Look for washed-out or overly dark prints. |
| Light test | Genuine cards block light in a consistent way. Do not rely on one test alone. |
| Weight and thickness | Compare to a known genuine card in the same sleeve type. |
| Slab checks | Case seams intact, label font and print match the grader, cert number matches the grader's online record, card in the case matches the record image where available. Case or label looks re-sealed: escalate. |
| Sealed product | Shrink wrap quality, seams, cuts, glue marks, weight against a known genuine unit, and printing. Resealed product is a serious concern. |

Compare any doubtful card side by side with the fakes the store keeps on hand, and with a known genuine copy, and refer to the counterfeit guide (in progress). If any check fails or you are unsure, do not complete the transaction. Pause politely, say you need a second look, and escalate to the Manager. Do not accuse or confront the customer (Agreement 6.4).

For detailed verification checks by item type (raw cards, sealed product, slabs, and language-specific checks), follow the Verification section of the Pricing & Buylist Guide.

### 4.1.1 Opening notes

- **Entering:** use the back door where you can, so no one can walk in through the front door while you are setting up. The front door stays locked until opening time.
- **Focus:** the first job is emptying the safe and stocking the front desk, shelves, and glass showcase. Showcases and displays and online orders come first (step 13).

- **Trash and recycling:** the key to the disposal room is in the safe, and the disposal room is in the parking garage outside. On disposal days (typically Monday and Friday), all of the bins are out the back behind the shop instead of in the underground garage, so it is easier to take out all of the trash and recycling then.
- **Arrival:** it is encouraged that both openers arrive by 11:15 am at the latest. If you are going to be late, make the opening person aware as early as you can, so they can plan the setup around it.
- **Staffing:** two team members always open the store together, and two always close it.

### 4.3.1 When closing can start

Closing can start as early as 1 hour before the official close time, depending on how busy the store is. Starting early spreads the work out and keeps the last minutes calm, but it never means closing the doors early. The doors stay unlocked and the counter stays staffed until the official close time, and customers can still shop and buy.

- Start early when the floor is quiet, few people are browsing, and no event, pickup, or buy is under way.
- Hold off, or start with a lower-risk task, when the store is busy or customers are crowded around the cases.
- If you are unsure, ask the Manager or Owner. The closer decides based on what they see on the floor.

### 4.3.2 Choosing how to start

Start with one or more of the three take-down tasks in 4.3.3, depending on how busy the store is and what type of customers are in it. These are starting points; use your judgement and keep the safety-first rule in mind.

| What you see | Good place to start |
| --- | --- |
| Quiet store; regulars or browsers who are nowhere near the cases | Clear the high-end items from the glass showcase and/or take out the slabs, while it is easiest to watch the cases. |
| A few customers, but none at the glass showcase or the slab case | Take down collection boxes and Pokémon Center ETBs from the shelves and the tops of the showcases, then return to the glass showcase or slabs when the floor quiets down. |
| A customer is looking at a particular showcase or slab | Leave that area alone until they finish. Work somewhere else. |
| Groups, unfamiliar or lingering customers, or people near the front door | Keep the glass showcase and slabs locked and stay near the front. Do low-risk take-down only, and delay high-end items until they leave. |

### 4.3.3 Take-down rules by item type

**Where things go:** high-end glass showcase items and the slab case go into the safe in the back room. Collection boxes and Pokémon Center ETBs go to the back room.

#### A. High-end items in the glass showcase

The glass showcase is near the front door, which makes it the easiest place for a grab-and-run. Be wary, and use one or both of these safe methods:

- **Clear it little by little.** Take out a few items, relock the showcase, carry the items to storage, then come back for the next few.
- **Watch it at all times while it is unlocked.** If you cannot keep your eyes on the case and the front door, relock it.

Other rules:

- Check the area first. If a customer is at or near the case, wait.
- Never leave items sitting on the counter or in your hands while you turn to a customer. Relock the case, or put the items straight into storage.
- Check that every item is accounted for as it goes into storage. If anything is missing, stop and follow the missing-item step in the runbook.
- With two closers, one watches the case and the front door while the other carries items.

#### B. Slabs from the showcase

Most of the slabs in the showcase are high-end items, so the same rules as the glass showcase apply: watch the case at all times while it is open, or work in small batches and relock.

- Remove the acrylic panels first and set them aside where they will not be scratched, knocked over, or in the way.
- Take the slabs out one by one and place them in order into the slab case. Keep the order so the slab case is easy to restock the next day.
- Hold slabs by the edges and keep them in your line of sight. Check each one for damage as it goes in (cracks, scuffs, a loose case).
- Close and lock the slab case, then put it in the safe in the back room.

#### C. Collection boxes and Pokémon Center ETBs

These do not have protective cases. A tear in the wrap devalues the product, so take your time.

- Lift with both hands and support from underneath. Do not grab by the wrap, squeeze the sides, or drag boxes across shelf edges, glass, or other boxes.
- Carry one or two at a time rather than a tall stack. Keep corners clear of doorways and fixtures.
- Put them down flat, with the heaviest at the bottom, stacked as high as they safely go (there is no set limit). Keep them away from liquids, sun, and anything sharp.
- Look at the wrap as you handle each box. If you see a tear or damage, stop. Do not tape it. Set it aside, photograph it, tell the Manager, and record it the same day (Section 10.6).

#### D. Booster boxes from the shelves

Once the glass showcase, the slabs, and the collection boxes and Pokémon Center ETBs are done, the booster boxes can come off the shelves.

- Booster boxes are sealed product, so protect the wrap the same way as in C: lift with both hands, carry small stacks, and do not drag them across shelf edges.
- Put them down flat in the back room, and keep them away from liquids and direct sun.
- If you find a tear or damage, stop, set the box aside, photograph it, tell the Manager, and record it the same day (Section 10.6).

### 4.3.6 Counting the cash

- Remove the cash from the drawer and count it with the cash counter in the back. Compare the counted cash plus any cash paid out for buys to the POS report.
- Log the final counts in the spreadsheet.
- **If there is a variance, troubleshoot where the discrepancy is.** Recount (with a second person if there is one). ### 4.3.7 Packing the safe

- The goal is to get the most dollar value into the safe that the space allows. Balance space against value: put in the highest-value items first (the slab case and the high-end glass showcase items), then fill the remaining room with the items worth the most for the space they take. Lower-value or bulky items go to the locked back room.
- At least one laptop, one iPad, and the phone typically go inside the safe.
- Pack it tightly but neatly. **An overstuffed safe may not close properly, and the lock might not engage.**
- Once everything is in, close the safe and check the lock engaged. Try to turn the handle, and try to pull the door open, several times. If it opens or the handle turns, the lock did not engage: take out the lowest-value items and try again.
- Check that every item is accounted for as it goes in.

### 4.3.8 Securing the store and leaving

- **The hole:** lock the hole gate, place the wooden board over the opening, and unplug the light to the hole. The board must sit flush with the wall, with any wires in front of the board.
- **Final sweep:** spend a minute or two looking over the whole store to make sure everything is packed up and off the floor and that the closing checklist was followed, before fully locking the back room.
- **Back room:** close the door and lock the gate to the back room. Make sure the curtain leading to the back is open, so the security cameras can capture anything that happens there while it is closed, in case of a break-in.
- **Exiting:** use the front door where you can. It is finicky and broken, so leaving through it is the best way to be sure the lock engaged. Lock the two back doors (they lock without a key), exit through the front door, and lock it with the key. Check that it is locked.
- **If you leave by a back door:** lock both back doors, then walk around to the front and check the front door is locked.
- **Alarm:** after everything is closed, message Leon that the store is cleared and empty so he can set the alarm (4.3.9).

### 4.3.9 Messaging Leon at the end of the night

There are two messages. Send the first as soon as everything is locked up, and do not hold it back to write the second.

**1. The all-clear.** After everything is closed, message Leon that the store has been cleared, that no one is inside, and that everyone has left. This is so he can set the alarm. Only send it once you have done the final sweep and know you are the last one out.

**2. Updates for the day.** Send these during this time, as a list, with anything that applies:

- **Low inventory:** a list of products the store is running low on, with roughly how many are left.
- **Procedures to change:** anything in a procedure that did not work well, or that might need to change, and why.
- **Incidents:** anything that happened in the store that day. Include what happened, the date and time, items and approximate value, people involved (description only, no guesses), and what staff did (Section 21.8).
- **Anything that needs his clarification:** pricing questions, buys on hold, or anything you were not sure how to handle.
- **Information from customers and connections:** anything that should be passed on from people in the community, such as suppliers, regular customers, or other contacts. Say who it came from, what they said, and whether they want a reply.
- **Variances and orders:** any till variance (Section 4.3.6) and any orders waiting.

Keep it short and factual, and do not include ID details, addresses, or payout amounts; refer to a record by date and buy number (Privacy Policy, Section 8). If there is nothing to report, say so.

Example:

> Store is cleared, no one is inside, everyone has left. You can set the alarm.
>
> Updates: low on \[product\] and \[product\]. One regular asked about \[item\] and wants a reply. A supplier mentioned \[info\]. No incidents. Till was on target.

## 5. Customer Service Standards

### 5.1 Standards

- Greet every customer within 10 seconds of entering, even when serving someone else (a nod, a wave, or "I'll be right with you").
- Be knowledgeable about the Pokémon TCG: current sets, release dates, popular chase cards, basic rules, and which products suit which customer. If you do not know, say so and find out.
- Be friendly, welcoming, and inclusive to players, collectors, parents, and new customers of all ages.
- Make recommendations when asked. Ask what they collect or play and their budget, then suggest a small set of options. Do not pressure.
- Stay professional on price and condition. Do not argue. You may explain how the price was reached (market source, condition, demand).
- Follow the Code of Conduct at all times. Prices, buylist offers, and trade values are based on the card, its condition, and market data, never on who the customer is. Ask, don't assume, and watch behaviour, never who someone is.

### 5.2 Handling common situations

| Situation | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Customer disagrees with a price | Explain how it was reached. Offer the market source if asked. Do not haggle beyond your discount authority. If the customer wants an exception, offer to ask the Manager. Never promise a future price hold. |
| Customer wants to see a high-value card | Ask them to confirm they are interested, then retrieve it (Section 6.4). Stay with the card the whole time. |
| Parent and child shopping | Speak to the child as a customer. Be clear on sealed-product final sale. Do not recommend products beyond a family's stated budget. |
| Customer is upset or loud | Stay calm and lower your voice. Listen without interrupting. Say what you can do. If still unresolved, bring in the Manager. If you feel unsafe, step back and call the Manager or 911. |
| Customer asks for something you cannot do (price hold, discount, off-policy return) | Say so kindly, give the reason, and offer what you can do. Escalate to the Manager if they ask. |
| Harassment or discrimination toward staff or customers | Do not tolerate it. Follow Section 10 of the Code of Conduct: stop it, separate and support, escalate, remove if necessary, and follow up. Record it on an incident report (Section 24.4). |
| Customer asks about a card or product you cannot find | Check the system and back stock. Offer a special order or wish list note (Section 6.7). |
| Customer asks to buy a high-demand product above a limit | Follow the release-day limits (Section 13). Do not make exceptions without the Owner. |

### 5.3 Customer messages

Try to answer messages and emails received through store channels within 1 to 3 days. Use a friendly, short reply. Do not share another customer's information. Escalate complaints and anything legal-sounding to the Manager.

### 5.4 Replying to emails

Replying to an email is the same as good customer service in the store, just in writing. Be friendly, professional, and clear, and use good judgement.

- **Use the premade drafts.** There are premade drafts for replying to common issues, such as inventory issues. Start from the matching draft, then check the customer's name, the order or item, and any details before you send it. It is also recommended to draft replies with Claude or Gemini, then modify the response to fit the situation.
- **Answer what they asked.** Keep it short and clear, and say what happens next.
- **Be honest and consistent.** Do not promise anything you cannot do, such as holding a price. Refunds, returns, and shipping follow Sections 11 and 12.
- **Protect privacy.** Check the recipient before you send. Never share another customer's information, and do not put ID details, addresses, or payout amounts in an email unless the customer is the one asking about their own order (Privacy Policy, Section 8). If you draft with Claude or Gemini, do not paste personal information such as ID details, addresses, or payment details into them; add those details yourself afterwards.
- **Escalate when you need to.** Send complaints, anything you are not sure how to answer, and anything that sounds legal to the Manager (see 5.3).

### 8.4 Seller records and secondhand dealer requirements

For every purchase of used or secondhand goods, follow the seller-identification and record-keeping procedure and any requirements of applicable secondhand dealer laws (Agreement 6.3). - Seller's name and contact details, the type of ID checked, and the date and time.

### 8.5 Counterfeit, altered, and stolen goods

- Team members must not trade or sell from their personal collection to customers, or buy from customers on their own account, while on shift (Agreement 7.3).
- Buys and trades with minors must be fair, transparent, and never pressured. Slow down, explain values in plain terms, and never take advantage of a young customer's lack of price knowledge (Code of Conduct, Sections 5 and 6).

### 8.8 Buying Heavily Played / Damaged (HP/D) cards

Heavily Played and Damaged are combined into one condition in our stickering and labelling system (HP/D). The cards inside that one condition vary a lot, so do not price them all the same.

**How much and how bad matters.**

- A Heavily Played card can have a lot of whitening on the back and still be HP. But once a card is HP, it can also have a lot of whitening on the front too. That is a very different card.
- A Damaged card can have a little damage or a lot.
- Look at how much damage there is, how severe it is, and where it is (front or back), even though the card is quantified as HP/D.

**Price it by its actual condition.**

1. Use the recommended system price as the baseline.
2. Go down from there, up to 100% off, based on the quantity and severity of the damage. You can go past the 5% deviation limit on HP/D cards without approval.
3. Show the seller the specific damage that set the price, and give real reasons for the offer.
4. If a card is too damaged, it is simpler to reject it.

- Payment details change or do not match the seller's name.

### 9.2 Price sources

## 15. Security and Loss Prevention

### 15.1 High-value inventory

- Keep expensive cards and products locked when not actively shown.
- Limit staff access to secured inventory. Keys, safe codes, and alarm codes are shared only with approved staff and never written where customers can see them.
- Graded slabs over $200, sealed boxes over $200, and singles above $50 must be stored in locked cases or the safe and moved only as set out in the Loss-Prevention Procedure (Agreement 6.5).
- Photograph high-value items on intake and keep the photo record.

### 15.2 Theft prevention

- Monitor suspicious behaviour: groups that split up, people who linger at high-value cases, unusually bulky clothing, bags near the cases, repeated requests to see multiple items at once.
- Greet and engage; visible service is the best deterrent.
- Limit the number of items out at one time. Never hand a customer more than one high-value item at once.
- Report theft incidents immediately to the Manager.
- Do not physically confront suspected thieves (Agreement 6.7). Your personal safety comes before merchandise.
- Write down a description, time, and what was taken, as soon as it is safe, and complete an incident report.

### 15.3 Key, access, and alarm control

- Report any lost key, shared password, or unauthorised person in a staff area to the Owner and the Manager immediately (Agreement 8.4 and 8.8).
- Never prop doors open or leave back doors unlocked.
- Do not let customers or delivery people past the staff-only line without a team member present.
- Alarm codes are changed when a team member leaves, or any time access may be compromised.
- Staff do not set the alarm. Once everyone has left, tell the Owner so the alarm can be set.

### 15.4 Cameras and reviews

Camera footage is reviewed by the Owner or a Manager the Owner designates, for incidents and variances. Do not share footage or screenshots outside the store. Coverage and how long footage is kept follow the Privacy Policy (Section 6.3).

### 15.5 Loss-Prevention Procedure

The **Captured! Cards Loss-Prevention Procedure** is the authority on preventing, spotting, and responding to loss. Follow it for the safety-first rule, showcase and slab handling, sealed product and loose packs, store layout rules, price tags and POS controls, cash and payment controls, event controls, and the responses to theft, counterfeit, payment fraud, break-ins, and till variances. This manual does not restate it.

The one rule to keep in mind: no card is worth getting hurt over. Watch behaviour, never who someone is (Code of Conduct, Section 9). Never chase, block, grab, search, or physically detain anyone.

One exception: closing take-down under 4.3.3 may start up to an hour before close, with customers still in the store. Section 4.3.3 prevails for closing.

This manual has a single named document owner, who is accountable for its accuracy. An SOP without a named owner is not issued.

### 23.2 Review cycle

The manual is reviewed **every 4 months**, after any major operational change, and following any major incident or issue. The Agreement is reviewed at least every 12 months, or on any change to store policy or applicable law. Management may update procedures as needed to improve operations and customer experience. Changes are communicated to the team directly.

### 23.4 Return of property on departure

On termination or request, return all keys, access cards, uniforms, equipment, inventory, documents, and Confidential Information, and hand over access to any Employer accounts (POS, email, shared inboxes, marketplace, social). The Owner changes alarm codes and revokes access the same day (Agreement 12.5).

### 23.5 Acknowledgement

Each team member signs that they have read this manual, understand it, and will follow it. Signed acknowledgements are kept in the employee file.

## 24. Forms and Log Templates

Use these field lists to build paper forms, spreadsheet tabs, or POS custom fields. These forms record the date and, where an approval was needed, the approval reference.

| Form | Fields to record |
| --- | --- |
| 24.1 Till count and reconciliation | Date and shift; team member; opening count (float, no more than $2,000); closing count; buy payouts in cash; POS cash total; variance and cause; reported to Manager (time); signature |
| 24.2 Buy and trade-in record (StorePass buylist) | Buylist number and date; seller name; ID type checked where required; items (name, set, number, condition, quantity, cert number); value per item and total; cash or store credit and amount; approval reference if needed; staff member |
| 24.3 Hold slip | Item and SKU; customer or purpose; date and expiry; price and deposit; approval reference (allocated or high-demand product); Manager or Owner who wrote it |
| 24.4 Incident report | Date, time, and location; people involved; what happened (facts only); items affected and estimated value; actions taken; police or emergency services called, if any |
